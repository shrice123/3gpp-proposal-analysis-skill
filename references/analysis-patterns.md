# Analysis patterns

## Choose the smallest useful analysis

- **Meeting landscape**: distribution by Agenda/KI/Solution, company, disposition, and relationship-chain density; identify promising deep dives.
- **Topic or Solution comparison**: technical alternatives, company positions, common ground, disagreements, open issues, and meeting outcome.
- **Company view**: one company's proposals, meeting comments, changes across revisions, and differences from other companies.
- **Decision trace**: baseline, inputs, revisions, merge decisions, approved output, and which ideas are demonstrably retained.
- **Evolution across meetings**: proposition-by-proposition changes with time and confidence.
- **Risk/open-issue review**: unresolved dependencies, technical objections, safeguards, and missing evidence.

## Clarification policy

First produce a preview grounded in actual meeting data.

- If the request maps cleanly to one cluster, proceed and state the interpreted scope.
- If one ambiguity materially changes the result, ask one focused question and show the leading candidates.
- If several clusters are plausible, offer 3-5 data-derived choices. Each choice should include identifiers, approximate candidate count, principal companies, and why it differs.
- If the user requests all proposals, do not force narrowing. Produce a landscape, define analysis batches, and continue in stages.
- If nothing matches, show the attempted meeting resolution, terms, aliases, and closest observed candidates.

Useful choices may be generated by Agenda Item, KI, Solution/Solution Variant, company, disposition, explicit relationship chain, or high-frequency topic. Do not generate choices unrelated to the preview.

## Recommended answer structure

1. Interpreted scope and coverage.
2. Proposal/decision chain.
3. Company positions in a compact evidence matrix.
4. Consensus, disagreements, and evolution.
5. Meeting outcome versus still-open issues.
6. Confidence, gaps, and source locators.

Keep facts and inference visibly distinct. A polished narrative does not replace the evidence matrix.
